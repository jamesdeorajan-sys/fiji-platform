# Marau Stage 1 — sixth independent review, "RESUME OWNERSHIP FIX"

Codex independently verified 349/349 passing tests at commit `a0bf43b`. Round 5's Fiji-time correction and interrupted-claim visibility were accepted, but R01 (confirmation/reconciliation concurrency) remained open with two concrete, deterministic reproductions of gaps the round-5 fencing design left open. This round replaces that design with statement-level ownership enforcement — not another isolated pause-hook patch.

## Root cause of round 5's remaining gap

Round 5's fencing checked `confirmation_attempts.phase` via CAS, but **only at specific `advance()` call sites** — the actual resource-mutating statements (the movement-claim INSERT, the vehicle-allocation INSERT, the offer hold/fill transitions, and the final `deal_requests` write) happened **between** those checks. This left two real windows a concurrent `reconcile-confirmation` call could exploit:

1. **Pause BEFORE the `vehicle_allocations` INSERT, after phase `CLAIMING_VEHICLE`.** Reconcile fences the phase (nothing else has happened yet, so it finds no allocation and unwinds), then the resumed original — unaware anything changed — went on to create a **fresh, now-orphaned allocation** nothing would ever release, before its next `advance()` call finally caught the fencing and aborted. Result: `409 CONFIRMATION_SUPERSEDED`, but one orphan allocation remained; request `REQUESTED`, offer `ACTIVE`, movement claims zero.
2. **Pause BEFORE the `confirmation_attempts` (journal) INSERT, right after decision-claim acquisition.** Reconcile found no journal row at all and simply freed the claim (following the "claim without a journal is safely recoverable" logic) — allowing a competing decline to win a **fresh** claim and complete normally. The resumed original, still holding its OWN stale reference to the (now-deleted) claim, had no way to detect this before its journal INSERT (a plain, unconditional insert in round 5) succeeded regardless, and it then ran its ENTIRE flow to completion — including the final write, which **never checked its own affected-row count**. Result: decline `200`; original confirm also `200 CONFIRMED`; actual request `DECLINED`, offer `FILLED`, allocation and movement claim present — two contradictory "successes" against the same request, with real resources left behind by the loser.

Both are the same underlying flaw: **phase CAS in a separate statement does not fence the subsequent resource write.**

## Fix — statement-level ownership enforcement via `deal_decision_claims.attempt_token`

A new column, `attempt_token` (migration `0021`), on `deal_decision_claims` is now the **single ownership primitive** for an entire confirm attempt:

1. **Unique attempt token on claim acquisition, journal keyed by the exact same token.** Immediately after winning the decision-claim `INSERT`, the confirm handler establishes ownership via a CAS (`attempt_token: NULL → <a fresh token>`). That SAME token value is then used as `confirmation_attempts.attempt_id` — the journal literally **is** identified by the exact ownership token, not a separately-generated, loosely-associated id. "A claim without a journal" (ownership established, but the journal row itself never landed) is therefore always safely and unambiguously recoverable: reconcile looks up the journal by that exact token and, finding none, knows with certainty that nothing beyond claiming ownership could have happened yet.
2. **Ownership enforced INSIDE every resource-mutating SQL statement, not checked afterward.** The movement-claim INSERT, the vehicle-allocation INSERT (`vehicle_allocation.js#claimVehicleAllocation`, extended with an optional, narrowly-scoped `ownership` argument — the ordinary-booking caller never passes it, so its behaviour is completely unchanged), the offer hold/fill transitions (via a new `createOwnershipFencedStore` adapter — see below), the compensating releases/reverts, and the final decisive `deal_requests` status write **all** carry `AND EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM deal_decision_claims WHERE request_id = ? AND attempt_token = ?)` as part of their own WHERE clause. The ownership check and the mutation are the same atomic SQL statement — there is no separate check-then-act gap for a concurrent reconciler to race into. This is what makes hold/fill (and every other mutation) **unable to ever "succeed for real" once ownership has been taken away**: they simply never complete at all once fenced, rather than completing and being discovered stale afterward.
3. **Reconciliation takes ownership atomically using its own unique recovery token — never merely nulls the old one.** `reconcile-confirmation` generates its own `recoveryToken` and performs `UPDATE deal_decision_claims SET attempt_token = <recoveryToken> WHERE request_id = ? AND attempt_token = <the value just read>` (a proper NULL-aware CAS, since `attempt_token IS NULL` needs its own branch — SQL's `column = NULL` never matches). This single CAS is exactly what makes **concurrent reconcilers mutually exclusive**: two simultaneous reconcile calls both read the same old token, but only one CAS against that exact old value can ever succeed; the loser re-reads fresh state (`ATTEMPT_STILL_ACTIVE`, `ALREADY_TERMINAL`, or `ROLLED_BACK_TO_REQUESTED`, whichever is actually true) rather than assuming anything or acting a second time.
4. **Compensation verifies both current recovery ownership and ownership of the specific resource being undone.** Every compensating write (offer revert, allocation release, movement-claim delete) — whether run by the original attempt's own `rollback()` or by a reconciler's unwind — goes through the same ownership-fenced primitives, scoped to the exact `request_id`/`allocation_id`/`source_movement_id` involved. `releaseVehicleAllocation` now takes an optional `ownership` argument and reports whether its delete actually matched (`{ released: boolean }`), so a caller can distinguish "genuinely released" from "ownership had already moved on, nothing done" — this is what makes it structurally impossible to reset another attempt's offer or delete an allocation that isn't this recovery's own.
5. **Every affected-row count is checked**, including — the concrete missing piece from round 5 — the **final decisive `deal_requests` write itself**, which now runs `WHERE status = 'REQUESTED' AND EXISTS (ownership)` and throws immediately if `changes !== 1`, rather than firing the UPDATE and unconditionally reporting success regardless of whether it matched anything.

### The shared Issue #54 engine is untouched

`holdOffer`/`fillOffer` (`smart-return-trigger-fill/src/offers.js`) and the state-machine rules they enforce are **completely unchanged**. `createOwnershipFencedStore` is a narrowly-scoped adapter — it implements the exact same `casOfferStatus(offerId, expectedStatus, nextStatus, patch)` contract as the shared engine's own `db.js` implementation (the SAME expected→next transition, the SAME SQL shape), with the ONE addition of the ownership `EXISTS` clause in its WHERE. `holdOffer`/`fillOffer` are simply called with this adapter instead of the raw store for the confirm flow's ownership-sensitive calls; nothing in the shared engine's own source was modified.

## Both exact Codex repros — reproduced first, then proven fixed

`marau_codex_fixes_round6.test.mjs` reproduces both scenarios deterministically (single-threaded test-only pause hooks — `__TEST_PAUSE_BEFORE_ALLOCATION__` and `__TEST_PAUSE_AFTER_CLAIM_BEFORE_JOURNAL__`), asserting **complete database invariants** (request status, offer status, allocation count, movement-claim count, and decision-claim freed/retained state) — not just response codes:

- **Repro 1** (pause before the allocation INSERT, after `CLAIMING_VEHICLE`): reconcile now correctly finds nothing to unwind and frees the claim; the resumed original's OWN allocation INSERT then fails its ownership `EXISTS` clause outright (never creates the allocation in the first place) and aborts with `409 CONFIRMATION_SUPERSEDED`. Final state: request `REQUESTED`, offer `ACTIVE`, **zero** allocations, **zero** movement claims, claim freed — a fresh confirm afterward succeeds cleanly with exactly one allocation, no duplicate/orphan.
- **Repro 2** (pause before the journal INSERT, right after claim acquisition): reconcile correctly recognises "claim without a journal" (looked up by the exact token) and frees it; decline wins a fresh claim and completes normally (`200 DECLINED`); the resumed original's journal INSERT itself fails its own ownership `EXISTS` clause (0 rows) and aborts immediately, **before any resource-mutating statement ever runs** — never completing its flow, never reaching its final write. Final state: request `DECLINED` (decline's real, honest outcome), offer `ACTIVE` (never filled), **zero** allocations, **zero** movement claims, claim correctly retained (terminal decisions are never deleted, per round 4's established behaviour).

Additional coverage: the round-5 pause point (after the allocation commits) still closes correctly under the new design; a new pause point after the offer is `HELD` but before `FILLING_OFFER`; two concurrent reconcile calls on the same stalled attempt (exactly one performs the takeover, the other correctly detects it lost the race); a completely unrelated, separately-confirmed request's resources are provably untouched by reconciling a different, stalled one; and a direct, isolated check that the final write's own affected-row count is what prevents a false `CONFIRMED` even when nothing about ownership is involved (a request that reaches `DECLINED` through an entirely different path before the final write runs).
