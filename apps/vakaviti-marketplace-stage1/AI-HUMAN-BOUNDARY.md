# Vakaviti AI/Human Boundary — Stage 1

Objective: automate the maximum safe share of partner discovery, onboarding, product digitisation and support while reserving humans for trust, legal/compliance, binding commercial and money decisions.

## AI-first functions
AI should perform or assist by default:
- discover public operator candidates
- normalize names, categories and locations
- deduplicate likely operators/products
- summarize public source material
- extract candidate contact details
- extract candidate product descriptions
- extract candidate price/review/pickup claims with provenance
- identify missing information
- generate a provider-ready profile draft
- generate product draft records
- suggest transport attachment opportunities
- prepare provider outreach and follow-up drafts
- answer onboarding questions from verified policy/context
- ask the minimum missing questions
- convert provider replies into proposed structured updates
- classify support requests and route exceptions
- create translation/localization drafts
- identify stale facts and re-verification candidates
- generate CEO/provider opportunity summaries from measured data

## Human-only gates
A human decision is mandatory before:
- VAKAVITI_VERIFIED status
- acceptance/rejection of licences, permits, insurance or legal/compliance evidence
- activation/change of bank or payout destination
- binding commission/rate/commercial agreement
- production price override where source authority is disputed
- operator suspension for misconduct/fraud where material adverse action is involved
- refund/dispute decisions above automated policy limits
- final approval of high-risk safety claims
- production-domain cutover

## Human-on-exception
AI may proceed automatically when rules/evidence are clear, but creates a human gate when:
- duplicate resolution confidence is low
- public sources materially conflict
- a provider disputes an existing canonical fact
- price/policy semantics are ambiguous
- identity cannot be established confidently
- an operator requests unusual payment terms
- safety-sensitive product information conflicts
- review provenance is unclear

## Provider experience target
A legitimate operator should not need to fill a conventional long registration form. The system should present a pre-built candidate profile and ask the provider primarily to:
1. confirm identity/contact control;
2. approve/correct products and public facts;
3. supply only missing commercial information;
4. supply required verification evidence;
5. accept commercial terms;
6. choose supported payment/availability preferences.

Everything else should be machine-assisted.

## Trust rule
AI output is stored as candidate evidence/suggestions. It does not silently overwrite canonical commercial truth.
